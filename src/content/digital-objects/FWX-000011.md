# Overview

JenniCam was a personal lifecasting website created by Jennifer Ringley
while she was a student at Dickinson College in Pennsylvania in 1996.

The project used a webcam and automated publishing system to periodically
capture still images from Ringley's living space and make them available
through the World Wide Web.

Unlike conventional personal websites, JenniCam made the continuing
observation of everyday life its principal form of publication. Images
documented ordinary activities including sleeping, working, using a
computer, socializing, and domestic routines.

The camera remained active during aspects of Ringley's private life as
well. Consequently, the historical JenniCam corpus included material
depicting nudity and sexual activity alongside otherwise ordinary
documentation of daily life.

The project attracted substantial public and media attention and became
an influential early example of personal lifecasting, anticipating later
forms of reality media, social broadcasting, and continuous online
self-publication.

JenniCam remained operational for more than seven years before Ringley
discontinued the project on 31 December 2003.

# Object Scope

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau defines JenniCam as the web publication
and automated lifecasting system operated by Jennifer Ringley between 1996
and 2003.

Individual images transmitted through JenniCam constitute historical
outputs of the system but are not automatically treated as independently
catalogued Digital Objects or unrestricted public holdings.

This distinction permits the Bureau to document the technical and cultural
significance of JenniCam without assuming that all surviving material
should be reproduced through the public catalogue.

# Preservation Note

The original JenniCam service is no longer operational. Surviving evidence
is distributed across archived webpages, captured images, contemporary
reporting, interviews, and other historical documentation.

Because the original service continuously generated transient imagery over
a period of several years, surviving archival material should not be
interpreted as a complete record of the historical JenniCam publication.

Some surviving material may contain intimate imagery. Historical public
availability alone is not considered sufficient justification for
unrestricted republication by the Bureau. Access and reproduction of such
material should be evaluated separately from the public classification of
the catalogue record itself.

The Bureau therefore records JenniCam as a fragmentarily preserved
historical Digital Object and distinguishes preservation of evidence from
unrestricted republication of its constituent material.
