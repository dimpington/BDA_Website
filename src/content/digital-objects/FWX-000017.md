# Overview

Hampster Dance was a personal webpage created by Canadian art student Deidre
LaCarte and hosted through GeoCities.

The page presented repeated rows of animated images of hamsters and other
rodents accompanied by a continuously looping audio sample. Its deliberately
simple construction reflected the tools and visual conventions available to
personal website creators during the late 1990s.

Initially created as part of an informal competition to attract website
visitors, the page subsequently circulated widely through email, links,
newsgroups, and word of mouth.

Hampster Dance became a prominent example of material achieving widespread
recognition through person-to-person circulation on the Web before
large-scale social networking and media-sharing platforms became the dominant
mechanisms for online distribution.

The object is also significant as an example of the cultural environment
supported by GeoCities. A page produced by an individual user through a
general-purpose hosting service could acquire an audience far beyond the
community or personal context in which it originated.

The original GeoCities service is separately recorded as FWX-000004.

# Preservation Note

The original GeoCities-hosted Hampster Dance page is no longer available
through the operational environment in which it was created.

Copies, archived captures, mirrors, reconstructions, screenshots, and
derivative versions survive through multiple external sources. These
resources vary in their fidelity to the original page and may differ in
graphics, audio behaviour, links, layout, or later additions.

The Bureau therefore records Hampster Dance as a distributed archival
preservation case rather than assigning any individual surviving copy the
status of original object.
