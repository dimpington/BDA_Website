# Overview

GeoCities was established in 1994 as Beverly Hills Internet and developed
into one of the largest communities of the early World Wide Web. Users were
provided with space to construct personal websites, initially organized
within themed virtual neighborhoods.

The service became an important environment for personal publishing during
the expansion of public Internet access in the 1990s. Its user-created pages
documented hobbies, identities, fandoms, communities, personal interests,
technical experimentation, and everyday life on the early Web.

Yahoo acquired GeoCities in 1999. The principal English-language GeoCities
service was discontinued in October 2009, placing a corpus comprising tens
of millions of user-created pages at risk of disappearance.

Prior to closure, multiple independent preservation efforts captured
substantial portions of the service. These efforts produced overlapping
archival corpora rather than a single complete surviving instance of
GeoCities.

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau treats GeoCities as an aggregate Digital
Object representing the historical hosting service and its surviving
archival corpus. Individual GeoCities websites may be assigned separate FWX
identifiers where their historical, cultural, or technical significance
warrants independent cataloguing.

# Preservation Note

No single surviving archival corpus is considered a complete continuation
of the original GeoCities service. Preserved material is distributed across
multiple external archival projects and varies in completeness,
presentation, and accessibility.

The Bureau therefore records the object as a distributed archival
preservation case rather than as an active or reconstructed service.
