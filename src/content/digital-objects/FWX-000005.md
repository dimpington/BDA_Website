# Overview

The Trojan Room Coffee Pot was developed at the University of Cambridge
Computer Laboratory as a practical means of monitoring a communal coffee
machine from elsewhere within the laboratory.

Beginning in 1991, a camera provided networked images of the coffee pot so
that researchers could determine whether coffee was available without
travelling to the room in which the machine was located.

In 1993, the image service was connected to the World Wide Web. The coffee
pot could thereafter be observed by Internet users outside the laboratory
and became widely recognized as the world's first webcam.

The service remained operational until 2001, when the Computer Laboratory
relocated from its former central Cambridge premises to the William Gates
Building.

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau treats the Trojan Room Coffee Pot as a
networked imaging service rather than as a website or as the physical coffee
pot itself. The associated camera, coffee pot, software, images, and
contemporary documentation constitute evidence of the historical Digital
Object but are not individually equivalent to the service represented by
this FWX record.

# Preservation Note

The original networked imaging service is no longer operational. Its
historical state is represented through surviving images, technical and
institutional documentation, archived web material, and associated physical
artifacts.

The Bureau therefore records the object as a documentary preservation case
rather than as an active or reconstructed service.
