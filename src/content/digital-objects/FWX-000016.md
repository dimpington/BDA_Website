# Overview

NCSA What's New was an early World Wide Web discovery resource maintained by
the National Center for Supercomputing Applications during the rapid expansion
of public Web activity in the early 1990s.

Maintained by Marc Andreessen and Eric Bina, the page presented information
concerning newly available websites, network resources, software, and other
developments associated with the emerging World Wide Web.

At a time when the Web remained sufficiently small for significant new
resources to be announced through individual curated pages, What's New
provided users with a means of discovering material beyond resources they
already knew.

The page consequently occupies an intermediate position between manually
maintained lists of Web resources and the automated discovery and search
systems that followed.

Its documentary significance also extends beyond the resources themselves.
Contemporary entries provide evidence concerning when particular websites and
services became publicly visible to sections of the early Web community.

JumpStation, recorded separately as FWX-000014, was among the early web
services announced through NCSA's What's New material.

# Preservation Note

NCSA What's New no longer operates as a current discovery service.

Historical versions and associated material survive through institutional and
external archival sources. Surviving copies provide a partial chronological
record of the rapidly developing Web during the period in which the resource
was actively maintained.

The Bureau records the object as an archival record rather than as an active
legacy website because its original function depended upon continuing
curation of newly available network resources.
