# Overview

The Space Jam website was published as an online promotional companion to
the 1996 Warner Bros. film Space Jam.

The website contains information and promotional material associated with
the film presented through a collection of themed pages. Its visual
language reflects conventions of commercial World Wide Web publishing
during the mid-1990s, including image-based navigation, patterned
backgrounds, compact page layouts, and graphics designed specifically for
web presentation.

Unlike many promotional websites created for contemporary films and
commercial campaigns, the historical Space Jam website remained publicly
accessible long after the period for which it was originally produced.

Its continued availability consequently provides evidence not only of the
film's early online promotion but also of the design, structure, and
technical conventions employed by commercial websites during the expansion
of the public Web in the 1990s.

# Object Scope

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau treats the historical promotional
website associated with the 1996 film as the Digital Object represented by
this record.

The record does not encompass the film itself, later Space Jam productions,
subsequent promotional websites, or the wider online presence of Warner
Bros.

This distinction is particularly important where later web services or
redesigned websites occupy related domains. Continued association with the
same franchise or rights holder does not by itself establish continuity
with the historical Digital Object.

# Preservation Note

The historical Space Jam website remains publicly accessible and retains
the characteristic visual presentation and navigational structure
associated with its original period of publication.

The Bureau records the website as an active legacy site preserved in situ.
This designation reflects the continued availability of the historical web
object rather than an institutional reconstruction of a discontinued
website.

The designation does not imply that every underlying server, network
component, protocol configuration, or hosting environment remains
technically identical to that used in 1996.

The object is maintained under routine observation by the Office of Digital
Preservation.
