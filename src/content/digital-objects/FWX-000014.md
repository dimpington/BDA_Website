# Overview

JumpStation was a World Wide Web search service developed by Jonathon Fletcher
at the University of Stirling in Scotland during 1993.

The system began indexing the Web in December 1993 and combined three
functions that would become characteristic of later web search engines:
automated discovery of webpages by a web robot, construction of a searchable
index, and a web-based interface through which users could submit queries.

JumpStation indexed titles and headings encountered by its crawler rather
than the complete textual contents of webpages. Search results were returned
as lists of matching network resources.

The service operated during a period in which the rapidly increasing number
of publicly accessible websites was making manual discovery progressively
more difficult. Its architecture therefore represents an early attempt to
treat the Web as a corpus that could be discovered automatically and searched
through a centralized index.

Development ceased during 1994 after Fletcher left the University of
Stirling.

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau records JumpStation as a historically
significant search service rather than merely as the website through which
its search interface was accessed.

# Preservation Note

The original operational JumpStation service is no longer available.

Surviving evidence consists primarily of contemporary references, technical
descriptions, later historical documentation, and archived material
associated with the service and its development.

The absence of the original searchable index and operational crawler prevents
direct reconstruction of the service as it existed during its active period.

The Bureau therefore records JumpStation as a documentary preservation case.
