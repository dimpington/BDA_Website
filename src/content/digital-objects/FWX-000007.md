# Overview

Napster was introduced in 1999 as a networked system for locating and
exchanging digital files between Internet users. The service became
particularly associated with the distribution of music encoded in the MP3
format.

The original Napster architecture combined centralized and peer-to-peer
components. Participating clients reported information about available
files to Napster-operated indexing infrastructure. Users searched this
centralized index to locate material, while the selected files were
transferred directly between participating computers.

This architecture distinguished Napster from both conventional centralized
download services and later peer-to-peer systems in which discovery itself
was distributed across participating nodes.

Napster experienced rapid adoption and became a major focus of legal
disputes concerning the unauthorized exchange of copyrighted recordings.
Litigation initiated by the recording industry in 1999 resulted in a series
of court orders requiring the service to restrict access to infringing
material.

The original Napster file-sharing network ceased operation in 2001.
Subsequent commercial services have operated using the Napster name, but
these systems are not considered continuations of the Digital Object
represented by this FWX record.

# Preservation Note

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau defines the original Napster Digital
Object as the integrated file-sharing system comprising the client
software, centralized discovery and indexing infrastructure, and
peer-to-peer transfer mechanisms.

Files exchanged by users through the service are not considered constituent
holdings of this record. The Bureau does not preserve or distribute
copyrighted recordings merely by virtue of their historical availability
through the Napster network.

The original network infrastructure is no longer operational. Its
historical characteristics are represented through surviving client
software, technical documentation, contemporary records, screenshots, and
other documentary evidence.

The Bureau therefore records the object as a documentary preservation case
and distinguishes it from later commercial music services operated under
the Napster name.
