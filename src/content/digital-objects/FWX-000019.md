# Overview

Cool Site of the Day was an early curated Web resource created by Glenn Davis
during the rapid expansion of the World Wide Web in 1994.

Rather than attempting to catalogue the Web comprehensively, the site
highlighted a single selected website each day. Visitors were directed from
the Cool Site of the Day page to a destination chosen by Davis, while earlier
selections were retained through an associated archive.

The resource was publicly announced by Davis in August 1994 at
`http://www.infi.net/cool.html`.

Its selective approach represented an early form of editorial Web discovery:
the value of the resource derived not from automated indexing, but from the
selection and recommendation of material considered noteworthy by its
maintainer.

During the following years the project expanded substantially. By 1997 it was
operating through InfiNet infrastructure at `cool.infi.net` and had developed
from its original two-page form into a larger publishing operation.

Cool Site of the Day is significant to the Bureau as an early example of
human-curated discovery during a period in which conventions for navigating
and evaluating the rapidly expanding public Web were still developing.

# Preservation Note

The original 1994 network location documented for Cool Site of the Day no
longer provides access to the historical Digital Object.

Later versions of the project operated at other network locations, including
locations associated with the name Cool Site of the Day. The domain
`coolsiteoftheday.com` has also been associated with later manifestations of
the project and remains relevant to its documentary history.

The existence of a currently accessible resource using the same name or an
historically associated domain is not, by itself, sufficient to establish
continuity with the Digital Object created in 1994.

Surviving archival captures, contemporary references and later documentary
sources preserve evidence of the site's historical operation, but the Bureau
does not presently treat any currently accessible representation as an intact
instance of the original operational resource.

The object is therefore recorded as historically unavailable with partial
preservation through archival and documentary evidence.

Questions concerning later network locations and possible continuity,
replacement or reconstruction remain subject to provenance examination.
