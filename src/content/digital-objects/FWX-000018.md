# Overview

ALIWEB, an abbreviation of Archie-Like Indexing for the Web, was an early
World Wide Web indexing and search system developed by Martijn Koster while
working at Nexor.

The system was announced in 1993 and subsequently presented at the first
International World Wide Web Conference.

Unlike crawler-based search systems, ALIWEB did not attempt to discover and
describe websites automatically. Participating website administrators could
instead provide structured index information describing resources available
through their sites.

ALIWEB periodically collected this submitted information and incorporated it
into a searchable index.

The approach reduced the need for automated crawlers to repeatedly retrieve
material from remote web servers, but depended upon website administrators
actively preparing and registering information for inclusion.

ALIWEB therefore represents a distinct early approach to the problem of Web
discovery: rather than automatically interpreting the expanding Web, it
provided a mechanism through which publishers could describe their own
resources for inclusion in a common searchable catalogue.

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau distinguishes this model from the
crawler-based approach represented by JumpStation and the later full-text
retrieval model represented by WebCrawler.

# Preservation Note

The historically significant ALIWEB search service is no longer treated as
an operational search system.

Contemporary descriptions, technical documentation, archived webpages, and
historical captures provide evidence of the system's design and public
presentation.

The original value of the object depended upon the combination of submitted
site descriptions, the maintained central index, and the search service
operating over that index. Surviving webpages alone do not reproduce that
operational environment.

The Bureau therefore records ALIWEB as a documentary preservation case.
