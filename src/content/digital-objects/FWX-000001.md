# Overview

The Million Dollar Homepage was created in 2005 by British student Alex Tew
as an Internet-based fundraising project intended to contribute toward the
cost of his university education.

The website presented a fixed advertising grid comprising one million
pixels. Advertising space was sold in blocks at a price equivalent to one
US dollar per pixel, with purchasers supplying small images and links to
websites that were displayed within the grid.

The unusual concept attracted substantial online and media attention.
Increasing publicity generated further advertising sales and transformed
the project into a widely recognized example of viral Internet marketing.

The resulting page also became a visual record of commercial web culture
during the mid-2000s. Its advertising grid contains contributions from a
large number of independent purchasers representing businesses, websites,
online services, personal projects, and other contemporary Internet
activity.

# Object Scope

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau treats The Million Dollar Homepage as
the website and advertising grid created and operated by Alex Tew.

The external websites linked from individual advertisements are not
considered constituent Digital Objects of this record. Their continued
availability may vary independently from the preservation state of The
Million Dollar Homepage itself.

This distinction is significant because the historical website may remain
accessible even where individual destinations represented within its
advertising grid have subsequently changed or disappeared.

# Preservation Note

The Million Dollar Homepage remains publicly accessible at its historical
domain and retains the characteristic presentation of the original
advertising grid.

The Bureau records the website as an active legacy site preserved in situ.
This designation describes the continued public availability and legacy
presentation of the Digital Object and does not imply that every external
resource linked from the page remains operational or unchanged.

The object is maintained under routine observation by the Office of Digital
Preservation.
