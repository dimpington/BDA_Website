# Overview

Cameron's World is an interactive web artwork created by Cameron Askin
from material recovered from archived GeoCities websites.

The project assembles text, graphics, animated images, hyperlinks, and
other elements originating from numerous GeoCities pages into a continuous
web collage. Rather than reconstructing an individual GeoCities website or
the GeoCities service itself, Cameron's World creates a new Digital Object
from fragments of the preserved GeoCities corpus.

The project presents these materials within a deliberately constructed
visual environment intended to evoke the heterogeneous and highly personal
character of World Wide Web publishing during the 1990s.

Development of the interactive website also involved Anthony Hughes, who
provided JavaScript and backend development, and Robin Hughes, who created
its accompanying music.

# Relationship to GeoCities

Cameron's World depends directly upon the preservation of GeoCities
material.

Following the closure of the principal GeoCities service in 2009,
independent archival projects preserved substantial portions of its
user-created websites. Cameron's World draws text, images, graphics, and
other material from these surviving archives and reorganizes those
fragments within a newly authored work.

The Bureau therefore treats Cameron's World as a Digital Object distinct
from GeoCities rather than as a reconstruction or surviving instance of
the original service.

Its relationship to FWX-000004 is nevertheless fundamental: the archived
GeoCities corpus constitutes source material from which Cameron's World
was constructed.

# Preservation Note

Cameron's World remains publicly accessible as an active web artwork.

The object incorporates historical material originating from numerous
earlier websites, but the presence of those materials within Cameron's
World does not establish the artwork as an authoritative reconstruction
of the original GeoCities service or of any individual GeoCities page.

Links within the project may direct users to independently preserved
historical webpages, and the availability of those external resources may
vary separately from the continued operation of Cameron's World.

The Bureau records Cameron's World in situ as a contemporary Digital Object
whose historical significance derives in part from its reuse and
interpretation of preserved digital heritage.
