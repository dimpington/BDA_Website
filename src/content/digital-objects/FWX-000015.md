# Overview

The Palace is a graphical networked communication system developed from a
concept created by Jim Bumgardner and released publicly during the mid-1990s.

Unlike text-oriented chat systems, Palace servers present users with graphical
rooms in which participants are represented by movable graphical avatars.
Users communicate within these shared spaces while interacting with visual
environments, other participants, and user-created graphical elements.

The system represents an early form of visually mediated online social space.
Its significance lies not solely in the Palace client software or individual
servers, but in the combination of client, server, graphical environment,
user identity, and community activity required to produce the operational
experience.

Official support for the original software eventually ceased. The underlying
protocol and software environment nevertheless continued through independent
clients, servers, directories, and communities maintained by users and
developers.

For catalogue purposes, the Bureau treats The Palace as a distributed
networked Digital Object whose historical identity encompasses both the
software system and the communities established through it.

# Preservation Note

No single surviving Palace server or contemporary client is considered a
complete preservation of the historical Palace ecosystem.

Continued operation depends upon independently maintained software,
community-operated servers, surviving technical knowledge, and compatibility
with modern computing environments.

The Bureau therefore records the object as a distributed community
preservation case.

Preservation assessment should distinguish between continued compatibility
with the historical Palace environment and reconstruction of individual
communities or servers that no longer survive.
