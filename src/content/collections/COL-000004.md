## Overview

The growth of the World Wide Web provided individuals with an increasingly
accessible environment in which to publish, experiment, communicate, and
construct personal forms of digital expression.

Outside institutional and commercial web publishing, users developed
distinctive visual conventions and cultural practices shaped by the
technical possibilities of contemporary browsers, hosting services, and
online communities.

This collection brings together GeoCities, Bert is Evil, and Cameron's
World as evidence of that vernacular Web: a digital environment created
not only by organizations and professional publishers, but by individuals
participating directly in the construction of online culture.

## Building the Personal Web

GeoCities provided hosting and community structures through which large
numbers of users could establish their own presence on the World Wide Web.

The resulting websites varied enormously in subject and purpose. Personal
homepages, fan sites, collections of links, hobby pages, community
resources, and experimental designs existed alongside one another within
an environment in which authors frequently learned the conventions of web
publishing through participation itself.

These pages developed a recognizable visual language. Background images,
animated graphics, counters, badges, banners, decorative typography, and
dense collections of hyperlinks became characteristic elements of a Web
constructed by its users.

The historical importance of GeoCities therefore extends beyond the
hosting service itself. Its surviving archival corpus records the creative
activity of a large and diverse population of early web publishers.

## Digital Folklore

Bert is Evil demonstrates another characteristic of the vernacular Web:
the ability of independently created material to circulate, mutate, and
acquire meanings beyond those intended by its original creator.

Created by Dino Ignacio in 1997, the website combined the familiar Sesame
Street character Bert with images and situations that deliberately
contrasted with the character's established context.

As material from the website was copied, modified, redistributed, and
encountered by new audiences, the work participated in forms of cultural
transmission that would later become closely associated with Internet
memes.

Its historical significance therefore lies not only in the original
website but in the wider processes of copying, transformation, and
redistribution characteristic of participatory Internet culture.

## The Web as Archival Material

Cameron's World represents a later stage in the history documented by this
collection.

Created by Cameron Askin in 2015, the web artwork draws upon material
recovered from archived GeoCities pages and reassembles elements of the
personal Web into a new digital composition.

The relationship is significant. Material originally created as part of
the living Web had, by this period, become an archival corpus from which a
later creator could construct a new Digital Object.

Cameron's World therefore demonstrates how preserved web material can move
beyond historical documentation and become source material for subsequent
creative work.

## Curatorial Note

The Vernacular Web does not define a single visual style or unified online
community.

The term is used here to describe forms of web publishing and cultural
production that developed through widespread individual participation,
informal experimentation, reuse, and circulation rather than exclusively
through institutional or professional publishing structures.

GeoCities represents the environment in which enormous quantities of such
material were created. Bert is Evil demonstrates the circulation and
transformation of independently produced online culture. Cameron's World
demonstrates the later reinterpretation of preserved vernacular web
material as historical and artistic source material.

Together, the objects document a cycle of digital culture:

creation, circulation, loss, preservation, and reinterpretation.
