## 1. Director's Statement

The preservation of digital heritage presents a particular institutional
challenge. Digital Objects may remain widely accessible for many years and
then disappear without notice. Others survive only in fragments, through
archival captures, obsolete software, retained storage media, or documentary
evidence of systems that no longer operate.

The work of the Bureau of Digital Antiquities is therefore concerned not only
with the preservation of digital material, but with the preservation of
context. A Digital Object cannot be adequately understood through surviving
files alone. Its network location, technical environment, provenance,
relationship to other systems, and history of public use may each form part
of the documentary record.

During 2025, the Bureau continued its programme of identifying, documenting
and assessing historically significant Digital Objects. Particular attention
was given to material associated with the early public World Wide Web,
network services whose original operational environments are no longer
readily reproducible, and surviving websites that continue to operate at or
near their historical network locations.

The year also saw continued development of the Forgotten Web Exchange as the
Bureau's principal catalogue of documented Digital Objects. Work undertaken
across the Bureau improved the consistency of catalogue records, strengthened
the recording of provenance and preservation status, and identified material
requiring further examination or recovery action.

The preservation of digital heritage is necessarily continuing work. No
catalogue can be considered complete, and no assessment of a networked
Digital Object should be regarded as permanently settled. Objects change,
infrastructure disappears, documentary evidence is recovered, and new
technical methods permit earlier conclusions to be reconsidered.

The Bureau enters 2026 with an expanded documentary base and a continuing
obligation to make that work available for study.

## 2. Bureau Mandate

The Bureau of Digital Antiquities is responsible for the identification,
documentation and preservation of historically significant Digital Objects.

The Bureau's activities include:

- identification and assessment of historically significant digital material;
- maintenance of documentary and provenance records;
- examination of surviving network-accessible Digital Objects;
- recovery and reconstruction activity where original access has been lost;
- preservation assessment and continuing observation;
- maintenance of archival collections and associated documentation; and
- publication of records and research for public study.

The Bureau does not regard preservation as synonymous with duplication.

Where historically significant material remains operational and publicly
accessible, continued observation and documentation may provide a more
appropriate preservation response than immediate reconstruction. Where an
original object has been lost or materially altered, archival evidence,
technical reconstruction and documentary preservation may instead be
required.

Preservation action is determined according to the condition, provenance,
accessibility and historical significance of the individual Digital Object.

## 3. The Year in Review

Bureau activity during 2025 was characterised by continued catalogue
development, examination of surviving legacy websites, review of historical
network locations, and consolidation of documentary material associated with
previously identified Digital Objects.

Several areas of work received particular attention.

The continued survival of websites originating during the early commercial
and public expansion of the World Wide Web provided opportunities for direct
observation of Digital Objects within their native medium. These objects
remain of particular preservation interest because their historical
significance is represented not only by their visual content but by their
continued existence as network-accessible systems.

At the same time, examination of older catalogue material identified cases in
which recorded network locations no longer provided access to the documented
object. Such cases required distinction between temporary inaccessibility,
domain or infrastructure migration, deliberate removal, replacement by
unrelated material, and complete loss of the original network resource.

The Bureau also continued the consolidation of provenance information.
Historical addresses, surviving archival captures, contemporary references,
technical documentation and other evidence were reviewed where available to
establish a more complete documentary history for individual objects.

These activities informed continuing development of Bureau preservation
procedures entering 2026.

## 4. Forgotten Web Exchange

The Forgotten Web Exchange remained the principal catalogue through which the
Bureau recorded historically significant Digital Objects during the reporting
period.

Catalogue work during 2025 concentrated on the quality and continuity of
Digital Object Records rather than the accumulation of records alone.

Review activity included:

- confirmation of historical and current network addresses;
- review of object names, dates and descriptive information;
- recording of operational and preservation status;
- identification of surviving archival or reconstructed access points;
- consolidation of provenance information;
- identification of relationships between Digital Objects; and
- referral of records requiring specialist examination.

Particular attention was given to the distinction between the historical
existence of a Digital Object and its present accessibility.

The loss of an original network location does not remove an object from the
historical record. Likewise, the continued operation of a domain does not
necessarily establish the survival of the Digital Object formerly located
there.

The Forgotten Web Exchange therefore records both surviving and unavailable
objects. Where appropriate, catalogue records may identify archival,
reconstructed or successor access points without representing those resources
as the original object.

A programme of further catalogue review was authorised for continuation
during 2026.

## 5. Digital Preservation

Digital preservation activity during the year included observational review
of surviving websites, assessment of archived material, documentation of
technical dependencies, and examination of objects whose continued
accessibility could not be established.

Surviving legacy websites presented a recurring preservation question.

Where an object continues to operate at a historical or successor network
location and retains substantial characteristics of its earlier form,
intervention may introduce greater alteration than continued observation.
Such objects may therefore remain under observation while their accessibility,
integrity and historical characteristics are periodically reassessed.

This approach does not imply that an operational website is technically
unchanged.

Changes in hosting infrastructure, transport security, browser behaviour,
external dependencies and routine maintenance may alter the environment in
which a Digital Object operates. Preservation assessment must distinguish
between such changes and alterations that materially affect the historical
identity of the object.

Work undertaken during 2025 contributed to the formalisation of procedures
for the continued verification of active legacy websites. Further technical
guidance arising from this work was scheduled for publication during 2026.

## 6. Recovery Operations

Recovery activity was undertaken where catalogue review identified Digital
Objects for which original public access had been lost, substantially
degraded, or could no longer be reliably established.

Recovery work may draw upon multiple forms of evidence, including surviving
files, archival captures, historical network records, contemporary
documentation, software dependencies, retained storage media and third-party
references.

No single source is assumed to provide a complete representation of a lost
Digital Object.

During the reporting period, several candidate objects were referred for
further examination following the discovery of incomplete or inconsistent
surviving material. In other cases, sufficient documentary evidence was
available to clarify the historical identity of an object without attempting
technical reconstruction.

One continuing investigation concerned a previously documented web resource
whose original network location remained active but no longer presented the
historical material associated with the Bureau record. Surviving captures and
contemporary references were retained for comparative examination.

The investigation remained open at the close of the reporting period.

Findings arising from recovery examinations may be published separately where
they are considered of wider preservation or research value.

## 7. Records and Provenance

The reliability of the Bureau catalogue depends upon the distinction between
documented evidence, reasonable inference and unresolved information.

Records and provenance work during 2025 therefore concentrated on improving
the evidential basis of Digital Object Records.

Sources reviewed during catalogue work included:

- surviving network resources;
- archived web captures;
- contemporary publications and references;
- technical documentation;
- historical domain and address information;
- retained digital files and metadata; and
- earlier Bureau documentation.

Conflicting evidence was not automatically resolved by selecting the most
recent source. Where the available record remained uncertain, that
uncertainty was retained in the relevant documentation.

This principle is particularly important in the study of early networked
material. Publication dates, authorship, original addresses and technical
characteristics may be incompletely documented, and surviving copies may
reflect different stages in an object's operational history.

Further work to standardise provenance notation and documentary references
was approved for 2026.

## 8. Bureau Archives

The Bureau Archives continued to maintain documentary and technical material
supporting preservation, research and catalogue activity.

Archival holdings include material retained as evidence of historically
significant Digital Objects as well as administrative and research records
generated through Bureau activity.

During 2025, archival work included the organisation of material associated
with existing catalogue records and the identification of holdings requiring
additional provenance review.

Where archival copies of publicly accessible material are retained, the
existence of those copies does not automatically change the preservation
status of the corresponding Digital Object.

An archival capture, screenshot, recovered file set or technical
reconstruction may provide significant evidence while remaining distinct from
the original networked object.

The Bureau will continue to maintain that distinction in both archival and
catalogue records.

## 9. Research and Publications

Research undertaken during 2025 supported the development of preservation
guidance, catalogue procedures and field examination methods.

A principal area of study concerned the treatment of Digital Objects that
remain publicly accessible despite their historical age.

This work considered questions including:

- when continued operation constitutes meaningful historical continuity;
- how changes in network location should be documented;
- how temporary inaccessibility should affect operational status;
- the evidential value of contemporary archival captures;
- the distinction between an original object and a reconstruction; and
- the circumstances in which direct preservation intervention is warranted.

The results of this work informed several documents scheduled for public
release during 2026.

The Bureau also continued preparation of material intended to make its
research and preservation methodology more readily available outside the
organisation.

## 10. Institutional Development

During 2025, the Bureau continued to standardise procedures used across its
offices and divisions.

Particular emphasis was placed on consistent terminology and the relationship
between catalogue, archival, preservation and recovery records.

The terms **Operational Status**, **Preservation Status**, **Historical
Address**, **Current Address**, and **Digital Object Record** were subject to
continued internal review to reduce ambiguity between technical condition and
documentary classification.

Work also continued on improving the relationship between the Forgotten Web
Exchange and supporting Bureau documentation.

These measures are intended to ensure that catalogue records can be revised
as new evidence emerges without obscuring the documentary history of earlier
assessments.

## 11. Priorities for 2026

The Bureau has established the following priorities for the 2026 operating
year:

1. continue review and expansion of the Forgotten Web Exchange;
2. formalise procedures for periodic verification of active legacy websites;
3. undertake field examination of selected Digital Objects;
4. develop additional guidance concerning preservation status and network
   continuity;
5. continue recovery examination of unavailable and materially altered
   Digital Objects;
6. improve the recording of provenance and relationships between Bureau
   records;
7. expand the organisation and description of archival holdings; and
8. establish improved public access to Bureau catalogue records, publications
   and institutional information.

The final priority represents a significant development in the Bureau's
public programme.

Historically, access to Bureau material has been distributed across individual
records, publications and archival resources. Work planned for 2026 will seek
to provide a more consistent public point of access to the Bureau's principal
documentary services.

Initial public access will necessarily represent only a portion of the
Bureau's holdings. Material will be released progressively as catalogue,
provenance and documentary review permits.

## 12. Administrative Statement

This report records the principal activities and institutional priorities of
the Bureau of Digital Antiquities for the year ending 31 December 2025.

References to continuing investigations, candidate Digital Objects and
unpublished Bureau material are included for administrative context and
should not be interpreted as final preservation determinations.

Catalogue classifications and preservation assessments remain subject to
revision where additional evidence becomes available.

The Bureau will continue its programme of identification, documentation,
preservation and public study during 2026.
