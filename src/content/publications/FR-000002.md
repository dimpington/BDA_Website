# Examination Summary

The Recovery Operations Division has completed a field examination of the
historical network locations associated with the Web resource known as
*Cool Site of the Day*.

The examination concerns a discrepancy first retained for further
investigation during Bureau verification activity in 2025. The historical
Digital Object was known to the Bureau through contemporary documentary
references and surviving archival material, while network locations
subsequently associated with the resource no longer provided an unambiguous
instance of the object documented in the historical record.

The case was retained as unresolved pending further examination of network
location, provenance and surviving representations.

Following adoption of revised Bureau procedures for the assessment of
historical network locations and reconstructed Web resources, the case was
reopened for formal examination in 2026.

The Bureau has confirmed the historical identity and significance of the
resource and has identified surviving documentary and archival evidence.
Continuity between the original resource and a contemporary website using the
name *Cool Site of the Day* has not, however, been established.

The case therefore remains unresolved with respect to continuity of the
currently accessible resource.

# 1. Object Under Examination

The object under examination is *Cool Site of the Day*, recorded by the
Forgotten Web Exchange as FWX-000019.

Cool Site of the Day was established by Glenn Davis in 1994 as a curated Web
resource presenting a selected website to visitors each day.

Contemporary documentation from 1994 identifies the resource at:

`http://www.infi.net/cool.html`

The site's function was editorial rather than algorithmic. Sites considered
noteworthy were selected and presented to visitors, providing an early form
of human-curated Web discovery during a period of rapid growth in publicly
accessible Web resources.

By 1997 the project had expanded substantially and was operating through
InfiNet infrastructure under the `cool.infi.net` network location.

The Bureau therefore distinguishes between the historical Digital Object and
any single network address through which that object was accessible during
its operational history.

# 2. Origin of Examination

The present examination originates in routine verification work undertaken by
the Bureau during 2025.

During that work, personnel reviewing previously documented historical Web
resources identified a case in which a network location associated with Cool
Site of the Day remained relevant to the historical record but did not provide
an unambiguous continuation of the Digital Object previously documented by
the Bureau.

Surviving archival captures and contemporary references were retained for
further examination.

The investigation remained open at the conclusion of the 2025 reporting
period.

The case was subsequently selected for renewed examination because it
presented several issues addressed by procedures formalised by the Bureau
during 2026:

- distinction between a Digital Object and its network location;
- reassignment or later reuse of historically associated locations;
- identification of original and reconstructed Web resources;
- use of archival captures as evidence;
- and treatment of unresolved provenance.

# 3. Historical Identification

Contemporary evidence establishes that Cool Site of the Day was operating by
August 1994.

The resource was created by Glenn Davis and initially consisted of a small Web
site through which a different selected resource was presented each day.

A contemporary December 1994 description identified Davis as the site's
volunteer webmaster and described the resource as a collection of selected
Web destinations accessible through the `www.infi.net` location.

Later contemporary reporting demonstrates that the project expanded
considerably.

By November 1997, Cool Site of the Day was described as an eight-person
publishing operation receiving millions of page views per month. Reporting
from that period identified the site through `cool.infi.net` and documented
InfiNet's intention to divest the publication.

These records establish both the historical existence of the object and the
fact that its network presentation changed during its operational history.

# 4. Historical Network Locations

The earliest network location confirmed during this examination is:

`http://www.infi.net/cool.html`

Later contemporary evidence associates the resource with:

`cool.infi.net`

The difference between these locations is not treated by the Bureau as
evidence of a separate Digital Object.

Network migration, restructuring and changes in hosting arrangements may
occur while an underlying Digital Object remains continuous.

For that reason, the Bureau records network location as provenance information
rather than using URL identity alone as proof of object identity.

# 5. Surviving Visual Evidence

A historical representation dated 1996 survives through later documentary
and archival sources.

The representation examined by the Bureau is consistent with the known
period of operation of Cool Site of the Day and provides evidence concerning
the visual presentation of the resource during that period.

The representation is useful as documentary evidence but is not itself
treated as a complete preservation of the operational website.

A screenshot records a rendered state of a resource at a particular time. It
does not independently preserve server behaviour, linked resources, navigation
paths or the complete technical environment in which the object operated.

The Bureau therefore uses the surviving image as corroborating evidence rather
than as a substitute for the historical Digital Object.

# 6. Archival Evidence

Archival representations provide additional evidence that Cool Site of the
Day existed at historically associated network locations.

Such captures are treated in accordance with Bureau citation and attribution
procedure.

An archival representation may preserve substantial portions of a Web
resource while nevertheless omitting externally hosted files, executable
components, server-side behaviour or resources excluded from capture.

Accordingly, the presence of archival material establishes evidence of a
historical state but does not by itself demonstrate uninterrupted operational
continuity between that state and a later resource.

# 7. Current Network Examination

A contemporary website is accessible through:

`https://coolsiteoftheday.com/`

At the time of examination, the resource identifies itself as *Cool Site of
the Day* and presents selected websites to visitors.

The contemporary resource therefore exhibits a functional resemblance to the
historical project.

Name and functional resemblance are relevant evidence. Neither is sufficient
on its own to establish provenance.

The Bureau has not identified evidence sufficient to demonstrate that the
currently accessible implementation represents uninterrupted continuation of
the resource created by Glenn Davis in 1994.

The Bureau likewise has not established that the contemporary implementation
is a restoration derived from historical site materials.

# 8. Identity and Continuity Assessment

The principal question in this case is not whether a website called *Cool Site
of the Day* presently exists.

It does.

The question is whether that resource represents the same Digital Object
documented in the historical record.

Assessment under TN-000003 requires the Bureau to distinguish among an
original resource, a modified original, a restored resource, an archival
representation, a mirror, a reconstruction and a recreation.

The evidence examined establishes:

1. the existence of the historical Cool Site of the Day;
2. its operation during the 1990s;
3. changes in historical network location;
4. surviving archival and documentary representations;
5. and the existence of a contemporary website using the same name and a
   closely related curatorial concept.

The evidence examined does not establish a sufficiently documented chain of
custody, ownership, technical continuity or restoration history connecting
the contemporary implementation to the original resource.

The Bureau therefore does not classify the contemporary site as an original,
modified original or restored instance of FWX-000019.

# 9. Network Location Assessment

TN-000002 requires network location to be assessed independently from Digital
Object identity.

In this case, several locations are associated with the history of Cool Site
of the Day.

The earliest documented `www.infi.net` location forms part of the provenance
record of the historical object.

The later `cool.infi.net` location forms part of the subsequent operational
history of the same project.

The contemporary `coolsiteoftheday.com` location is relevant to the later
documentary history of the name and concept.

The Bureau has not established that the presence of a resource at the
contemporary location constitutes migration of the original Digital Object.

The contemporary location is therefore recorded as a location requiring
provenance qualification rather than as the verified current address of the
historical object.

# 10. Evidentiary Limitations

Several limitations affect the present examination.

The Bureau does not possess a complete record of every hosting arrangement,
ownership transition, transfer or implementation associated with Cool Site of
the Day between its establishment in 1994 and the contemporary resource.

Surviving archival captures are incomplete representations of the original
operational environment.

Contemporary reporting establishes important portions of the resource's early
history but does not provide a continuous provenance chain extending to the
present implementation.

Similarity of title, purpose and presentation cannot substitute for such a
chain.

In accordance with PM-000002, absence of sufficient evidence is recorded as
uncertainty rather than converted into an assumption of either continuity or
discontinuity.

# 11. Findings

The Recovery Operations Division records the following findings.

**Historical identity:** Confirmed.

Cool Site of the Day existed as a distinct Web resource created by Glenn Davis
in 1994.

**Historical network location:** Confirmed.

Contemporary evidence identifies `http://www.infi.net/cool.html`, with later
operation documented through `cool.infi.net`.

**Surviving historical evidence:** Confirmed.

Documentary references, archival representations and historical visual
material survive.

**Original operational resource:** Not presently accessible as an intact
verified instance.

**Contemporary resource using historical identity:** Accessible.

A contemporary website using the name *Cool Site of the Day* and a similar
curatorial function is publicly accessible.

**Continuity with the historical Digital Object:** Unresolved.

Evidence examined by the Bureau is insufficient to establish that the
contemporary resource is an original, modified original or restored instance
of the historical Digital Object.

# 12. Catalogue Action

FWX-000019 shall remain recorded as a historical Digital Object.

Its earliest confirmed historical network location shall remain recorded as:

`http://www.infi.net/cool.html`

The object's operational status shall remain:

**Historical Resource Unavailable**

Its preservation status shall remain:

**Partial Archival Preservation**

The contemporary `coolsiteoftheday.com` resource shall not presently replace
the historical address as the object's verified current network location.

It may instead be retained within the provenance record as a contemporary
resource requiring qualification.

No finding of continuity or discontinuity shall be inferred solely from the
continued use of the historical name.

# 13. Disposition

The 2025 investigation is closed as a field examination.

The underlying provenance question remains unresolved.

Further examination may be undertaken if documentary evidence becomes
available concerning ownership transfer, source material, restoration history,
hosting continuity or other provenance connecting the historical resource to
the contemporary implementation.

Any such evidence shall be evaluated against the procedures established in
TN-000002, TN-000003 and PM-000002.

Until that time, the Bureau shall maintain a distinction between:

- the historical Digital Object;
- its documented historical network locations;
- surviving archival representations;
- and the contemporary resource using the same name.

This distinction preserves the evidentiary record without asserting continuity
that has not been demonstrated.
