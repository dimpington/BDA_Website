## 1. Purpose

This standard establishes the Bureau of Digital Antiquities' requirements for
the identification, citation and attribution of source material used in public
institutional records.

Digital preservation frequently depends upon evidence assembled from material
created at different times, retained under different conditions and accessed
through different technical environments.

A surviving website, archival capture, screenshot, contemporary publication,
technical document or earlier Bureau record may each provide evidence about a
Digital Object. None should be represented as establishing more than the
source itself reasonably supports.

The purpose of citation is therefore not solely to identify where information
was obtained. Citation forms part of the Bureau's record of provenance.

## 2. Scope

This standard applies to source material used in:

- Digital Object Records;
- Field Reports;
- Preservation Bulletins;
- Technical Notes;
- Publications Manuals;
- Annual Reports where detailed source attribution is required;
- Collections and associated curatorial material; and
- other public Bureau documentation where factual or documentary claims depend
  upon identifiable external evidence.

The level of citation should be proportionate to the nature of the record.

A short catalogue description does not require the citation apparatus of a
formal Field Report. Material supporting significant historical,
preservation or provenance determinations should nevertheless remain
identifiable within the Bureau's documentary record.

## 3. General Principle

A citation should permit a reader, examiner or later Bureau officer to
understand the source upon which a statement or determination was based.

Where practicable, a citation should identify:

1. the creator, publisher or responsible body;
2. the title or descriptive name of the source;
3. the date of publication or creation, where known;
4. the relevant network location or archival reference;
5. the date on which a network resource was accessed or verified, where
   material to the record; and
6. any qualification necessary to explain the condition or provenance of the
   source.

Information that cannot be established should not be invented to complete a
citation.

The absence of a known date, author or original address should be recorded as
uncertainty where that absence is significant.

## 4. Categories of Source Material

Bureau research may rely upon several categories of documentary evidence.

These include:

- **Primary network resources** — surviving websites, services or other
  Digital Objects observed directly at a public network location;
- **Archival captures** — preserved representations obtained through a web
  archive or other archival system;
- **Contemporary documentation** — publications, announcements, manuals,
  journalism or other material produced during the operational period of the
  object;
- **Technical documentation** — specifications, software documentation,
  source material, configuration records or other technical evidence;
- **Visual records** — screenshots, photographs, scans or recorded
  demonstrations;
- **Retained files and metadata** — digital files, filesystem information,
  embedded metadata or other retained technical material;
- **Later historical sources** — retrospective research, interviews and
  secondary accounts; and
- **Bureau records** — earlier catalogue entries, reports, examination notes
  and institutional documentation.

The category of a source does not by itself determine its reliability.

Sources should be assessed according to their provenance, proximity to the
matter being documented, internal consistency and relationship to other
available evidence.

## 5. Surviving Network Resources

Where a Digital Object remains publicly accessible, direct observation may
provide evidence of its present condition.

The record should distinguish present observation from historical fact.

The fact that a website displays a statement, date or attribution does not
necessarily establish that the displayed information has remained unchanged
since original publication.

Where the continued accessibility of the resource is material to a Bureau
determination, the date of observation or verification should be recorded.

A current network resource may support statements concerning its present
content, structure and accessibility. Historical claims derived from that
resource should be evaluated separately.

## 6. Historical Network Addresses

Historical network addresses should be recorded exactly enough to identify the
resource under examination.

The Bureau should distinguish between:

- an original or historically documented address;
- a later address used by the same Digital Object;
- a redirect;
- an archival address;
- a reconstructed or mirror location; and
- a former address now occupied by unrelated material.

The present existence of a domain name does not establish continuity with the
Digital Object historically associated with that domain.

Where a former address has been reassigned or its content materially replaced,
the address may remain relevant as historical evidence but should not be
presented as a current access point to the original object.

## 7. Archival Captures

An archival capture is evidence of material preserved or observed by an
archival system at a particular point in time.

It is not automatically equivalent to the original Digital Object.

When an archival capture is material to a finding, the record should identify,
where practicable:

- the archive or repository;
- the captured network address;
- the capture date;
- the archival access location; and
- the date on which the capture was examined by the Bureau.

Limitations should be acknowledged where relevant.

A capture may omit external resources, scripts, images, media, server-side
behaviour or other dependencies present in the original operational
environment.

The existence of a capture therefore establishes evidence of preserved
material, not necessarily a complete representation of the object as it
functioned at the time.

## 8. Screenshots and Visual Evidence

A screenshot documents a visible presentation observed under particular
conditions.

Where an external screenshot is reproduced by the Bureau, its source should be
identified where practicable.

Where the Bureau creates the screenshot directly, the attribution should make
that distinction clear.

A screenshot should not be used as sole evidence of functionality that cannot
be established from the image itself.

For example, an image of a navigation control may demonstrate that the control
was visually present. It does not by itself demonstrate that the control
functioned.

Likewise, a screenshot should not be represented as the Digital Object itself.

## 9. Contemporary and Secondary Sources

Contemporary sources may provide evidence unavailable from surviving Digital
Objects.

Such material may be particularly important when establishing:

- original publication dates;
- creators or responsible organisations;
- historical network addresses;
- intended function;
- contemporary reception;
- technical characteristics; or
- the relationship between related systems.

Later secondary sources may assist interpretation and historical
reconstruction but should not automatically displace contemporary evidence.

Where a later account conflicts with contemporary documentation, the
difference should be examined rather than silently reconciled.

## 10. Conflicting Evidence

Conflicting evidence is a normal condition of digital historical research.

The Bureau should not resolve a conflict solely to produce a simpler public
record.

Where one source is considered more reliable than another, the basis for that
assessment should be identifiable from the available evidence or examination.

Where the conflict cannot reasonably be resolved, the publication should
preserve the uncertainty.

Suitable language may include:

- "reported as";
- "documented by";
- "appears to have";
- "the available record indicates";
- "the Bureau has not independently established"; or
- "the surviving evidence is inconclusive."

Such qualifications should communicate genuine evidential limits rather than
serve as routine editorial disclaimers.

## 11. Attribution

Attribution identifies the creator, custodian, publisher, archive or other
responsible source associated with material used by the Bureau.

Attribution must not imply ownership by the Bureau where none exists.

External images, archival captures and reproduced material should identify
their source where practicable.

Bureau-created screenshots may be described using a form such as:

**Screenshot captured by the Bureau of Digital Antiquities**

where that description accurately represents the origin of the image.

The source of the underlying Digital Object should be identified separately
where necessary.

## 12. Citation of Bureau Records

Bureau publications should cite other Bureau records using their permanent
identifiers.

On first significant reference, the identifier should ordinarily be
accompanied by the title.

For example:

**PM-000001 — Bureau Editorial and Style Manual**

or:

**FWX-000002 — Space Jam Website**

Where the context remains clear, subsequent references may use the identifier
alone.

Relationships recorded through the Bureau publication or catalogue system
should supplement rather than replace meaningful references within the text.

## 13. Evidence and Bureau Assessment

Publications should distinguish between what a source records and what the
Bureau concludes from that evidence.

A source may establish that a network address was advertised at a particular
date.

An archival capture may establish that particular material was observable by
the archival system at another date.

A contemporary article may document how a service was described publicly.

The Bureau may use those sources collectively to reach an assessment of the
identity, continuity, condition or significance of a Digital Object.

The assessment remains a Bureau determination and should not be attributed to
the sources unless those sources themselves make the same determination.

This distinction is particularly important where a Preservation Status,
Operational Status or recovery finding depends upon interpretation of
multiple incomplete sources.

## 14. Unavailable and Lost Sources

The loss of a source does not necessarily invalidate an earlier documented
observation.

Where a source previously examined by the Bureau is no longer publicly
available, the record should not silently replace its former address with an
unrelated current resource.

Existing citations may be retained as historical references.

Where an archival copy or successor source is identified, it may be recorded
in addition to the historical citation provided that the distinction is
clear.

The Bureau should avoid presenting an archival substitute as though it were
the originally cited source.

## 15. Editorial Review

Source references should be reviewed as part of the publication process.

Review should consider:

- whether the source supports the associated statement;
- whether observation and inference are distinguished;
- whether historical and current addresses are clearly identified;
- whether archival captures are described accurately;
- whether reproduced material is appropriately attributed;
- whether uncertainty has been retained where necessary; and
- whether relationships to other Bureau records are correctly identified.

A technically valid link is not sufficient evidence that a citation is
editorially appropriate.

Likewise, a broken historical link is not sufficient reason to remove a
citation from the documentary record.

## 16. Maintenance of this Standard

This standard is maintained by the Publications Office under the general
editorial framework established by **PM-000001 — Bureau Editorial and Style
Manual**.

Revisions should preserve the distinction between editorial presentation and
the evidential requirements of Bureau research.

Changes affecting substantive citation or attribution requirements should be
recorded through revision control.
