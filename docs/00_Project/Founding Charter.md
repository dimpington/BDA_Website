## Foundational Principles

### Principle I — Establishment

The Bureau of Digital Antiquities was established in 1998 as a small public institution dedicated to preserving historically significant artifacts of the early Internet before they were permanently lost.

---

### Principle II — Scope of Responsibility

The Bureau documents historically significant digital heritage regardless of whether it is lost, archived, dormant or still actively maintained.

---

### Principle III — Principle of Documentation

The Bureau describes digital heritage objectively rather than evaluating it.

Documentation shall distinguish observation from interpretation and preserve historical accuracy above narrative.

---

### Principle IV — Institutional Conduct

The Bureau communicates with the objectivity, precision and professionalism expected of a public archival institution.

Publications shall avoid sensationalism, speculation presented as fact, and emotionally charged language.

---

### Principle V — Preservation Before Interpretation

The Bureau preserves evidence before interpretation.

Original material, provenance and historical context shall be documented prior to analysis or assessment.

---

### Principle VI — Universal Historical Value

Every digital object possesses historical context worthy of preservation regardless of its scale, popularity or continued operation.

Historical significance shall be determined through documented context rather than contemporary relevance.
